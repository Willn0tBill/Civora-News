A loan's interest rate is not its entire price.

Annual percentage rate includes more borrowing costs than the quoted interest rate, which can change how loan offers compare.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-10
BusinessWhat APR AddsC·

A lender advertises a low interest rate, but the paperwork lists a higher annual percentage rate. That is not necessarily a typo. The figures answer related but different questions about what borrowing can cost.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that a mortgage interest rate reflects the charge for borrowing principal, while APR is a broader measure designed to include certain additional charges and costs. The details of what is included vary by the type of credit and applicable disclosure rules.

Comparing two lenders using only the advertised interest rate can conceal differences in fees. On the other hand, comparing APR alone may not fully describe the cost of a loan that is repaid or refinanced early, because some charges are paid upfront.

Loan duration matters. An annualized percentage does not mean the borrower will necessarily pay that fraction of the original balance every year. Amortization, balance changes and payment schedules shape actual dollar costs.

Fixed-rate and adjustable-rate offers introduce another complication: the rate may change under the loan's terms. A disclosure based on assumptions is not a guarantee of future market conditions.

The practical comparison asks for the same loan amount, term and type of rate, then examines APR, upfront charges, monthly payments and total expected costs. A smaller percentage in a headline is not always the less expensive deal.
