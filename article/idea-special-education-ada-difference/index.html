Education rights can come from more than one federal law.

IDEA, Section 504 and the ADA protect students in different ways; one school accommodation conversation may involve several legal standards.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-11
Courts & Civil RightsEducation rights can come from more than one federal lawC·

Parents hear that a student qualifies for services under one federal law, then encounter another law in a school meeting. The terms can sound interchangeable, but they are not.

The Individuals with Disabilities Education Act provides a framework for special education and related services for eligible children. Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act and the Americans with Disabilities Act address disability discrimination and access under different standards.

An individualized education program, commonly called an IEP, is associated with IDEA eligibility and specific educational goals and services. A Section 504 plan is not simply an alternate name for the same document.

Who qualifies, what support is required and how disputes are resolved depend on the particular legal framework and facts. General summaries do not substitute for a student's documented evaluation and applicable rights.

The strongest education coverage names the legal basis, the agency responsible and the specific action under debate. 'The school refused accommodations' may be an important concern, but the facts and law are necessary to evaluate it.
