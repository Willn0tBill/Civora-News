2025 was cooler than 2024—and still an exceptionally hot year

NASA's annual temperature report shows why a single-year decline does not reverse decades of warming. The baseline and the bigger trend tell the story.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-09
ScienceThe Warming RecordC·

NASA's latest annual temperature report contains two facts that belong in the same headline. The world was cooler in 2025 than it was in record-setting 2024. It was also still exceptionally warm compared with the historical record. Leaving either part out gives readers an incomplete picture.

In its January 14, 2026 analysis, NASA put 2025's global average surface temperature 1.19°C above the agency's 1951–1980 reference average. The year was effectively tied with 2023 within measurement uncertainty, while 2024 remained the warmest in NASA's record dating to 1880.

The 1.19°C figure is an anomaly, not Earth's absolute temperature. It means the measured temperature was that much higher than a specific reference-period average. Comparing anomalies from different baselines without adjusting for the difference can create confusion rather than clarity.

NASA builds its analysis using records from more than 25,000 weather stations and observations from ships, buoys and Antarctic research stations. Researchers also work to address uneven geographic coverage and sources of bias. It is a statistical reconstruction, not one thermometer hanging in a single location.

Several independent research groups arrived at broadly consistent conclusions. NASA's release cites NOAA, Berkeley Earth, the Hadley Centre and Copernicus, which also ranked 2025 among the warmest years. Minor differences in close rankings reflect differing methods, not a disagreement that recent decades have warmed.

Climate is not expected to break a new annual record every year. Ocean conditions and natural variability affect individual years, and one exceptional peak can be followed by a slightly cooler year without changing the longer trajectory. This is why scientists examine much more than one adjacent-year comparison.

The number that matters depends on the question. If the question is whether 2025 topped 2024, the answer is no. If the question is whether the temperature record still shows substantial long-term warming, NASA's analysis says yes. Both findings can be true at once.

Reporting note: Civora analyzed NASA's published January 2026 report and temperature-indicator guidance. We did not collect independent temperature measurements.
