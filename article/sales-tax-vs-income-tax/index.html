A sales-tax rate tells you less about the total bill than you think.

Sales and income taxes apply to different bases and at different stages, making a single percentage a weak comparison of household burdens.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-10
BusinessTwo Tax BasesC·

A receipt adds sales tax to a purchase, while a paycheck shows federal or state income tax withholding. They are both taxes, but the percentages describe different bases and should not be added together as if they were one rate.

Sales taxes generally apply to covered transactions under state and local rules. The rate and whether a particular product or service is taxable can depend on the jurisdiction and exemptions. A headline rate does not describe every transaction.

Income taxes usually depend on measures of taxable income and the rules governing deductions, credits and rates. In graduated systems, a marginal rate describes the tax applied to the next unit of income, not necessarily the average paid across the whole amount.

Sales taxes can affect households differently because people spend different proportions of income on taxable consumption. Income taxes likewise depend on earnings, filing circumstances and the law applying to a particular taxpayer.

A comparison between states should account for local sales taxes, property taxes and other funding sources rather than assuming the absence of one tax means the government collects little revenue overall.

The most honest report identifies the tax base, jurisdiction, effective period and what an illustrative household or transaction actually pays. A percentage without the base is only half the arithmetic.
