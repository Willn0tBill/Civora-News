Your online data can travel much farther than the website where you entered it

Data brokers compile information from different sources. What a company knows about a person may be broader than one account or purchase.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-10
TechnologyThe Data TrailC·

Most people understand that a website can remember information entered into an account. The less visible part is what happens when information is combined with records from other services, transactions or publicly available sources.

Data brokers collect, aggregate and distribute information about consumers for a range of uses. The FTC has studied how these businesses build profiles, and privacy regulators have warned that people may have limited visibility into where those profiles came from.

A profile can become more revealing when several ordinary data points are connected. A location, an interest and a purchase history may tell a different story together than any one fact would alone. The exact practices vary by company and jurisdiction.

The presence of a name in a broker's database does not prove every associated inference is correct. Errors can spread, and algorithmic classifications can be mistaken for verified facts by the people who receive them.

Privacy settings on one app may reduce some sharing but do not necessarily control information already available elsewhere. Real understanding requires looking at the sources, recipients and rules governing particular data transfers.

The important question is not simply whether a company has your information. It is where that information came from, what the business is allowed to do with it and whether a person can inspect or correct relevant records.

Reporting note: Civora reviewed FTC data-broker investigations and consumer privacy resources. No company-specific data profile was obtained or tested.
