A Creative Commons label doesn't mean you can do anything with a photo.

CC licenses grant defined permissions while requiring users to respect attribution, commercial-use and derivative-work conditions where applicable.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-10
Media & PublishingThe Permission Behind the PhotoC·

A photographer releases an image under a Creative Commons license, and someone assumes it is available for any purpose without restrictions. That can be wrong. Creative Commons offers several standardized licenses that grant different permissions.

The Creative Commons organization explains how conditions such as attribution, noncommercial use, ShareAlike and NoDerivatives alter what a person may do with a work. These labels are not interchangeable, and a website's mention of 'CC' is not a complete license statement.

CC BY generally requires credit and permits broad reuse under its terms, while a ShareAlike condition can require adaptations to be shared under compatible conditions. NonCommercial and NoDerivatives terms impose other restrictions. Exact wording and the version of the license matter.

Public-domain tools and licenses also differ. CC0 is designed to waive certain rights to the extent possible, whereas an ordinary CC license uses copyright permissions. Neither erases potential privacy, publicity or trademark concerns that a particular image might raise.

Attribution is more than an unlabeled photographer name hidden at the bottom of a page. A good credit identifies the creator, title when available, source, license and meaningful modifications such as cropping.

The reliable approach is to read the file's license and source page rather than trusting an image-search thumbnail. A freely viewable picture is not necessarily free to republish.
