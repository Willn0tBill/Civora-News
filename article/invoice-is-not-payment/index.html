A sent invoice does not mean the seller has been paid.

Billing, revenue recognition and cash collection can occur at different times, affecting how healthy a business really is.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-11
BusinessA sent invoice does not mean the seller has been paidC·

A company completes work and sends an invoice for several thousand dollars. On paper, the sale may look finished. In the bank account, the money may not have arrived.

An invoice documents an amount billed under agreed terms. Depending on accounting methods and circumstances, revenue can be recognized before payment is collected.

Accounts receivable represent amounts customers owe. A growing receivables balance can reflect healthy sales, slow collections or increased credit risk; the number alone is ambiguous.

Businesses need cash to pay workers, rent and suppliers even when customers promise to pay later. This timing gap is especially important for smaller firms with limited reserves.

A useful story about business growth asks not only how much was billed but how much was collected and when. Sales momentum cannot be measured by invoices alone.
