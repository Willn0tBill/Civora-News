No, one cooler year does not mean global warming stopped

2025 came in below the 2024 record. NASA's measurements still place it among the hottest years observed—and the difference matters.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-09
ScienceOne Year Is Not a TrendC·

The claim sounds simple: 2025 was cooler than 2024, so global warming must be over. The first part is true. The conclusion is not. An annual drop in temperature does not erase a long-running trend, and NASA's latest published figures make that distinction clear.

In January 2026, NASA reported that Earth's average surface temperature for 2025 was 1.19°C above its 1951–1980 reference average. It was cooler than 2024, the warmest year in NASA's record, but remained effectively tied with 2023 within the analysis's uncertainty. That is not what a return to earlier climate conditions looks like.

The mistake is treating climate like a scoreboard where every year must beat the previous one. Natural variation can push one year above or below another. Scientists examine records across decades precisely because the underlying pattern cannot be determined by comparing just two years.

The reference period matters as well. A temperature anomaly measures the difference from a chosen baseline; NASA uses the average from 1951 to 1980. Someone who quotes a number without naming the baseline leaves out information readers need to understand it.

NASA also pointed to analyses by NOAA, Berkeley Earth, the Hadley Centre and Copernicus. Their methods are not identical, and close annual rankings may differ. But the independent records all place 2025 among the warmest years, consistent with continued long-term warming.

Verdict: Unsupported. The observation that 2025 was cooler than 2024 is accurate; the assertion that this proves global warming ended is not. A credible claim about climate change has to survive the full record, not just the two dates that make the strongest headline.

When a temperature graph goes viral, ask three questions: What data produced it? What years define the baseline? And is the argument about a single year or a sustained trend? Those checks are far more useful than declaring victory based on one downward step.
