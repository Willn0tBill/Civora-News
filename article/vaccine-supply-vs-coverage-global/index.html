A country can receive vaccines and still have low immunization coverage.

Supply, delivery capacity, records, access and confidence are separate links in reaching children and communities with vaccination.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-11
Humanitarian & DevelopmentA country can receive vaccines and still have low immunizatiC·

A shipment of vaccines arrives at an airport and a photograph captures a major logistics milestone. But delivered doses are not the same as people fully immunized.

Immunization coverage measures the share of a defined population receiving particular vaccinations. It depends on delivery systems, outreach, reliable records, trained staff and access to care.

The 'last mile' may be the hardest: cold-chain requirements, remote geography, conflict, staffing shortages or inconsistent services can prevent doses from reaching intended recipients. Missed appointments or gaps in follow-up can also matter.

Global coverage figures vary by vaccine, age group and reporting method. Comparing a shipment count with a coverage percentage without a shared denominator creates a misleading impression.

Stronger coverage tells readers which vaccine and population are measured, what services are available and whether coverage has improved over time. Procurement is necessary; successful delivery is the test.
