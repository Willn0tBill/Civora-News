A free trial can become a recurring bill before you notice

Automatic renewal is part of many subscriptions. The price and cancellation process deserve attention before the first payment is taken.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-10
BusinessThe Hidden RenewalC·

The word 'free' gets attention, but a trial offer can include an agreement to start charging later. The important detail is not simply what something costs today; it is what happens when the trial ends.

The FTC explains that subscription offers may use automatic renewal arrangements where a customer is billed unless they cancel. Those terms can be legitimate when properly disclosed and authorized, but confusing or deceptive practices create problems for consumers.

Some customers discover recurring charges after overlooking the renewal date or misunderstanding the cancellation process. In other cases, regulators have alleged that companies enrolled people or made cancellation unnecessarily difficult.

The business model relies on ongoing access rather than a one-time transaction. That means a monthly price, the length of any commitment and conditions for stopping payment all belong in the buying decision.

Consumers can check the written renewal terms and keep records of cancellation requests. It also helps to review statements for charges that continue after access was supposedly ended. The actual rights and remedies depend on the circumstances and applicable law.

A subscription is not automatically a bad deal. But an offer should be evaluated by its total expected cost, not only the attractive number at the top of the sign-up page.

Reporting note: Civora reviewed FTC consumer guidance on free trials and unwanted recurring charges. No specific company was independently investigated.
