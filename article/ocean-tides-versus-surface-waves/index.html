Tides and ocean waves can rise together while having different causes.

Tides are driven mainly by gravity and Earth's motion; ordinary surface waves are usually driven by wind.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-11

A beach can have high tide and rough surf at the same time. The two observations are related to coastal water but arise from different physical processes.

Tides are long-period changes in sea level caused mainly by gravitational interactions involving the Moon and Sun, together with Earth's rotation and local geography.

Most everyday surface waves are generated by wind transferring energy to the sea. Swell can travel far beyond the storm that created it, reaching a coastline under otherwise calm skies.

Storm surge adds another process: weather-driven changes in coastal water levels. A high tide combined with surge and large waves can create more dangerous coastal conditions than any one factor alone.

Marine reporting should distinguish water level, wave height and storm surge. They are not interchangeable measurements, even when they occur at the same beach.
