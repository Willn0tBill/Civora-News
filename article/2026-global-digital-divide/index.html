Two billion people remain offline as global internet access reaches 76%

The ITU says 6.3 billion people use the internet in 2026, but the newest figures reveal vast gaps in affordability and access.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-09
WorldThe Offline DivideC·

Internet access has passed a symbolic threshold, yet a connection remains out of reach for roughly two billion people. The International Telecommunication Union, the United Nations agency for digital technologies, estimated on November 3, 2026, that 6.3 billion people worldwide use the internet. That represents 76 percent of the global population, according to its Facts and Figures 2026 report.

The total was up from a revised 6.1 billion users in 2025. It signals continued expansion, but does not mean connectivity is universal or equally useful. Those who remain disconnected can face barriers to educational materials, job applications, public services and communication that increasingly assume digital access.

The global average conceals a striking income gap. The ITU estimates that 93 percent of people in high-income countries use the internet, compared with 32 percent in low-income countries. These figures describe actual usage rather than a simple count of mobile towers or cables, a distinction that matters when evaluating infrastructure claims.

Price remains part of the problem. According to the ITU, three in ten economies meet neither its fixed-broadband nor mobile-broadband affordability target. Even when a service is technically available, ongoing subscription charges, equipment costs and limited digital skills can prevent people from using it.

Quality is another dividing line. An unreliable shared connection may allow occasional messages but cannot support the same tasks as stable broadband. Being within cellular coverage is different from possessing a suitable device, affordable data and the skills to use online tools securely.

Readers should also compare statistics carefully. The ITU revised its estimate for 2025 upward from six billion to 6.1 billion internet users. Statistical revisions can reflect updated information and methods, so figures lifted from different editions should not automatically be treated as directly comparable.

The report therefore raises questions beyond coverage maps: Who can afford to connect, who has reliable service, and who benefits from new digital tools? The headline milestone is substantial, but the remaining exclusion is equally important.

Reporting note: This Civora News article is an original synthesis of the ITU's 2026 published statistics. No original interviews or field reporting were conducted.
