Culture / EXPLAINER

A cultural tradition can be protected without putting it behind glass.

UNESCO's intangible heritage framework covers living practices and knowledge, emphasizing communities and transmission rather than ownership of objects.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-10
CultureHeritage That LivesC·

A historic building can be photographed and placed on a preservation list. A dance, oral tradition or craft technique exists differently: it lives through people who learn, practice and reinterpret it.

UNESCO's Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage describes practices, expressions, knowledge and skills that communities recognize as part of their heritage, alongside associated instruments and spaces.

Safeguarding can involve documentation, education and conditions that support the practice's continued transmission. It is not simply a command to freeze a tradition in an invented 'original' form forever.

International lists can attract attention and support, but inclusion does not establish exclusive ownership by one government or prove that every person in a community practices a tradition in exactly the same way.

Communities may also debate what recognition means, who speaks for practitioners and how tourism or commercialization changes a custom. Those questions deserve reporting beyond a celebratory ceremony.

A careful story identifies the practitioners, how knowledge passes between generations and what threats or opportunities they actually describe. Living heritage is not a museum artifact with a single permanent label.
