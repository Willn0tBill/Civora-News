One veto can block a Security Council resolution. It cannot settle the underlying conflict.

The United Nations Security Council has 15 members, but five permanent members hold a veto over substantive decisions.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-10
WorldThe Power of a VetoC·

A United Nations Security Council resolution can gain overwhelming support and still fail. The reason is structural, not a mathematical mistake: five permanent members have a special voting power that can prevent adoption of substantive decisions.

The United Nations explains that the Council has 15 members. Nine affirmative votes are required for decisions, but on substantive matters a negative vote by any permanent member can block adoption. China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States hold that status.

A permanent member's abstention is not the same as a veto. If enough other members vote yes, a resolution can pass when a permanent member abstains. That distinction matters when headlines describe the role a particular country played.

Procedural decisions are governed differently, and the Council's authority varies depending on the Charter provision and issue. A debate, draft resolution and adopted decision should not be described as equivalent stages of collective action.

Even a blocked resolution does not make a conflict disappear. Governments, humanitarian agencies, courts and other international bodies can continue to act within their own powers. Likewise, an adopted resolution is not a guarantee that its goals will be implemented successfully.

A useful report names the draft, the vote tally, the permanent member's vote and what the text would actually have required. The veto explains why one decision failed; it is not a complete explanation of the crisis being debated.
