A shipping container's paperwork is part of international trade security.

Cargo declarations, standards and risk-based inspections support trade oversight; customs officials do not physically open every container.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-11

A port handles thousands of containers, each holding goods that may pass through multiple carriers and jurisdictions. Physically opening every box would be an enormous challenge.

International customs systems depend on declarations, documentation, data exchange, inspections and risk management. Authorities select shipments for closer examination based on laws, information and available resources.

The World Customs Organization's SAFE Framework addresses securing and facilitating the global supply chain. Maritime safety rules also govern how containers are handled and loaded.

A clean document does not prove a shipment contains exactly what it claims. Conversely, selecting a container for inspection does not prove wrongdoing. Both are reasons reporting should distinguish checks from findings.

Readers following a port disruption should ask which authority is responsible, what type of review is underway and whether it affects a particular route or category of goods. 'Cargo stopped' can describe very different situations.
