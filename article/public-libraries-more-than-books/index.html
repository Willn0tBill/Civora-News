A public library can be an internet connection, a study space and a community service.

Library value includes access to information, staff assistance and public space, not only the number of books circulated.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-11
CultureA public library can be an internet connection, a study spacC·

A city debates whether libraries are still needed because people can read on their phones. That argument treats a library as a shelf rather than a public institution.

Libraries may provide digital access, study areas, educational programs, research help and community meeting spaces in addition to physical collections. Offerings differ among systems and available budgets.

For people without reliable home internet or a quiet place to work, a library can provide practical access that a subscription service cannot. Staff expertise also helps people navigate information rather than merely retrieve it.

Circulation statistics remain useful but do not measure every benefit. Program participation, public computer use, hours and geographic access offer additional views of service.

Budget discussions should start by identifying what residents actually use and need. A decline in one form of borrowing does not mean every library function has become obsolete.
