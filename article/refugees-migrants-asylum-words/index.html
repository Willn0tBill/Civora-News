Refugee, migrant and asylum seeker are not interchangeable labels

The words describe different legal and practical circumstances. Using them carelessly can mislead readers about why people move and what protections apply.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-10
Humanitarian & DevelopmentWords That MatterC·

One news story calls someone a migrant, another calls the same person an asylum seeker, and a third uses refugee. The words are often treated as shorthand for anyone crossing a border. They are not the same.

The UN refugee agency describes refugees as people who have fled their country because of persecution, conflict or other serious threats and meet applicable protection criteria. An asylum seeker is a person seeking international protection whose claim has not yet been finally determined.

'Migrant' is a broader term commonly used for people who move from their usual residence, including for work, family or other reasons. It does not by itself say whether the person has a refugee claim, lawful immigration status or a right to remain in a particular country.

Legal frameworks differ by country, and journalists should not infer an individual's status from an image or a border-crossing video. Words have consequences: each label can suggest rights, procedures and facts that may not be established.

The distinction also helps explain why displacement statistics can differ. Counts of refugees, asylum seekers, internally displaced people and international migrants use different populations and reporting methods.

Readers deserve more than a single dramatic label. A responsible report identifies the specific group being counted, the governing definition and what remains unknown about individual circumstances. Accuracy here is not about euphemisms; it is about describing reality.

Reporting note: Civora reviewed UNHCR and International Organization for Migration terminology resources. No individual legal cases were assessed.
