A pay raise can be smaller than it looks after inflation.

Nominal wages show dollars paid; real earnings adjust for changes in consumer prices and reveal a different trend.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-11
Markets & EconomyA pay raise can be smaller than it looks after inflationC·

A paycheck rises, but groceries and rent also cost more. The employee may have more dollars while feeling little improvement in what those dollars buy.

Economists distinguish nominal wages, measured in current dollars, from real wages, adjusted for changes in prices. Real earnings help track purchasing power over time.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes measures of real earnings using price indexes. Results depend on which workers, earnings definition and inflation measure are used.

A national average also conceals differences among households. Individuals face different spending patterns, regions and benefit arrangements, so their experiences may not match a single headline number.

A wage story should state whether an increase is nominal or inflation-adjusted and specify the comparison period. Without that context, 'pay is up' can be true and misleading at once.
