Space junk can remain dangerous long after a mission ends.

Defunct satellites, spent rocket hardware and fragments can threaten functioning spacecraft because orbital collisions occur at high relative speeds.

BY Civora Research DeskPUBLISHED 2026-11-11
Space & AstronomySpace junk can remain dangerous long after a mission endsC·

A satellite stops operating, but its place in orbit does not instantly become empty. Hardware and debris can continue circling Earth for extended periods.

Orbital debris includes fragments from collisions or breakups, spent stages and inactive spacecraft. The risk depends on size, altitude, orbital pathways and the speed of encounters.

Tracking networks monitor many objects, but smaller fragments are harder to catalog. Even a small piece can pose a hazard at orbital speeds.

Operators use design, monitoring and disposal planning to reduce future risks. Debris-mitigation guidelines seek to limit accidental releases and prevent abandoned objects from worsening the environment.

Space sustainability should be judged over decades, not just by how many launches succeed this year. A mission's final responsibility includes what happens after it stops working.
